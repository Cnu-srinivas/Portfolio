# How I Introduce Myself

Last updated: 6 October 2026 · Srinivas Dharavath

## Core facts

Every introduction below is built from the same six facts. Pick the two or three that matter most to the person in front of you.

| Fact | How to say it in one line |
| --- | --- |
| Education | Dual Degree (B.Tech + M.Tech) from IIT Madras in Naval Architecture and Ocean Engineering |
| Current role | Senior AI Engineer, leading AI product builds end to end |
| What I build | LLM-powered conversational agents, multi-agent RAG systems, automated risk management pipelines, real-time voice survey systems |
| Tools | LangGraph, LangChain, CrewAI, OpenAI APIs, Python, Django REST Framework, Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD |
| Cloud | Deep expertise in AWS and Azure, serverless architecture, scalable microservices |
| Side work | Co-founder of MentionNow (AI visibility for brands), AI/ML teaching assistant to 800+ students |

The story in one breath: an engineer from IIT Madras who moved from ship design to AI, and now builds LLM systems that run in production on the cloud.

The three words to leave behind in any room: **IIT Madras**, **production AI**, **cloud architecture**.

## The 10-second version

Use one of these when you have only a handshake's worth of time. Say it slowly, then stop and let them ask.

| Setting | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Default | "I'm Srinivas. I'm a Senior AI Engineer from IIT Madras. I build LLM systems that run in production on AWS and Azure." |
| Casual meetup | "I'm Srinivas. I build AI agents and RAG systems for a living, and I co-founded a startup on the side." |
| Business room | "I'm Srinivas. I turn AI ideas into working products, from the first prototype to the cloud deployment." |
| Formal event | "Srinivas Dharavath, Senior AI Engineer. IIT Madras graduate. I specialise in agentic AI and cloud architecture." |
| Online bio | "Senior AI Engineer, IIT Madras. Building production LLM agents, RAG systems and cloud platforms. Co-founder, MentionNow." |

Each line has the same shape: name, one strong credential, one thing you build. Never add a fourth part to a 10-second intro.

## For HR and recruiters

They want to match you to a role fast. Give them the title, the years, the stack, and proof that you finish things.

**30-second intro**

"I'm Srinivas Dharavath, a Senior AI Engineer. I have a Dual Degree from IIT Madras and about four years in engineering, most of it on cloud and AI. I build LLM-powered agents, RAG systems and automation pipelines, and I take them all the way to production on AWS and Azure. My core stack is Python, LangGraph, LangChain, Django REST Framework, Docker and Kubernetes. I have also led AI product builds end to end, from requirements to go-live."

**If they ask for more**

- Experience in plain words: "I started as a backend and AWS engineer, moved into AI engineering, and now I lead AI product builds as a senior engineer."
- Team fit: "I've worked with product, backend and business teams, and I've taught AI/ML to 800+ students, so I'm comfortable explaining AI to anyone."
- What you are looking for: say the role type and the work style in one line. Example: "Senior or lead AI engineering roles, remote or hybrid, where I own a product."

**Keywords they listen for:** Senior AI Engineer, LLM, RAG, agentic AI, LangGraph, AWS, Azure, Kubernetes, CI/CD, production, end to end.

**Avoid:** project details, framework debates, and anything that needs a whiteboard.

## For founders

Founders want to know if you can ship fast and if you understand their pain. Lead with speed and with the fact that you are a founder too.

**30-second intro**

"I'm Srinivas. I'm a Senior AI Engineer and I co-founded MentionNow, an AI visibility platform for brands. My day job is building LLM agents and RAG systems that go live, not just demos. I've built conversational agents, multi-agent pipelines and a real-time voice survey system, all on AWS and Azure. I like working with founders because I've sat on both sides: writing the code and worrying about the product."

**If they ask for more**

- Speed: "I can take an AI feature from idea to a working prototype in days, and to a stable production version in weeks."
- Cost: "I design for the cloud bill from day one. Serverless where it fits, Kubernetes where it needs to scale."
- Your own startup: "MentionNow tracks how a brand shows up in answers from ChatGPT, Gemini and other AI engines, and helps improve it. We're at MVP stage."

**Questions to ask them:** "What part of your product is AI touching today?" and "What is breaking when real users hit it?"

**Avoid:** listing every tool you know. Founders care about outcomes, not your toolbox.

## For CEOs and business leaders

They think in results, risk and money. Drop the tool names. Talk about what the AI does for the business and that it works after launch.

**30-second intro**

"I'm Srinivas Dharavath, a Senior AI Engineer from IIT Madras. I build AI systems that do real work inside a business: answering customers, reviewing documents, running surveys, flagging risk. The part I'm known for is making them reliable. Many AI projects look great in a demo and fail in production. I design them for production from the start, on AWS and Azure, so they keep working when real users and real data arrive."

**If they ask for more**

- Business examples: "I've built an automated risk management pipeline, a voice survey system that calls people in real time, and AI assistants that handle customer conversations."
- Reliability: "Every system I build has guardrails, monitoring and a clear hand-off to a human when the AI is not sure."
- Leadership: "I own the whole delivery, from the first requirement to go-live and monitoring. Leaders get one person to talk to."

**One sentence to close with:** "If you tell me where your team is spending hours on repeated work, I can usually tell you in a day whether AI can take it over safely."

**Avoid:** acronyms like RAG or LLM without a plain-English version right after. Say "AI that reads your own documents before it answers" instead of "RAG".

## For tech leaders: CTOs, VPs, engineering managers

They judge depth and judgment. Show that you know the architecture, the trade-offs, and what breaks at scale.

**30-second intro**

"I'm Srinivas, Senior AI Engineer. I design and ship LLM systems end to end: stateful agent workflows in LangGraph, multi-agent RAG with CrewAI, and the backend around them in Python and Django REST Framework. I deploy on AWS and Azure, with Docker, Kubernetes and CI/CD, and I've run both serverless and microservice setups depending on the load. I've also fine-tuned open models for a domain-specific task. My focus is reliability: evaluation, guardrails, observability and cost control, so the system holds up after launch."

**If they ask for more**

- Architecture: "I write the HLD and LLD myself, then build it. I choose serverless for bursty, low-volume workloads and Kubernetes for steady, high-volume ones."
- Agents: "I treat agents as state machines, not magic. Every step is logged, every tool call is typed, and there is always a fallback path."
- Results: "One automation I built cut manual effort in email review and ticket creation by around 70%."
- Team: "I've mentored engineers and taught AI/ML to 800+ students, so I can bring a team up to speed on LLM engineering quickly."

**Questions to ask them:** "How are you evaluating your AI features today?" and "Who owns the AI system after it ships?"

**Avoid:** overselling any single framework. Tech leaders respect someone who says "it depends" and then explains on what.

## For developers and engineers

Developers want to know what you actually build and whether you are fun to work with. Be specific, be relaxed, and invite them into the details.

**30-second intro**

"Hey, I'm Srinivas. I'm an AI engineer. Most of my time goes into LangGraph agents, RAG pipelines and the Python backends that serve them. I've built a real-time voice survey system with Twilio and OpenAI, a multi-agent risk assessment pipeline with CrewAI, and a few conversational agents that are live in production. I deploy everything myself on AWS or Azure with Docker and Kubernetes. Before AI I was a backend and AWS engineer, so I still care a lot about clean APIs and boring, reliable infra."

**If they ask for more**

- Current interests: "Lately I'm deep into agent evaluation, MCP-based tool use, and keeping LLM costs down with smart routing and caching."
- Fine-tuning: "I fine-tuned LLaMA 3 8B for Indian legal text. It taught me more about data quality than about model weights."
- Side project: "I co-founded MentionNow. We check how brands show up in AI answers. Happy to show you the stack."

**Questions to ask them:** "What are you building?" then "What's the most annoying part of it?" Developers bond over shared pain.

**Avoid:** titles and credentials up front. Mention IIT Madras only if they ask where you studied.

## For clients and prospects

A client wants to know three things: can you solve my problem, will it keep working, and will it be painful to work with you. Answer all three in the first minute.

**30-second intro**

"I'm Srinivas Dharavath, a Senior AI Engineer from IIT Madras. I build AI systems that work after the demo. That means AI assistants that handle customer conversations, pipelines that read and check documents, voice systems that run surveys, and automation that takes repeated work off your team. I build them on AWS or Azure, with proper monitoring and a human fallback, so you are not left with something fragile. You work with me directly, from the first call to go-live."

**If they ask for more**

- Proof: "One automation I built reduced manual effort in email review and ticket creation by around 70%."
- Process: "We start with a short discovery call, then a small working version in a couple of weeks, then we scale what works."
- Safety: "Your data stays in your cloud account. I design for that from day one."

**Questions to ask them:** "Where does your team spend the most time on repeated work?" and "Have you tried AI for this already, and what happened?"

**Avoid:** promising numbers you have not measured for their case. Say "in a similar project we saw around 70%" and let the discovery call set the target.

## How to adapt on the spot

The same six facts, in a different order, make every version above. Use these rules when you meet someone who fits none of the groups.

1. **Open with the credential they respect most.** IIT Madras for HR, CEOs and investors. "Production AI" for tech leaders and developers. "Co-founder" for founders and investors.
2. **Say one thing you build, not five.** Pick the project closest to their world. Voice survey for sales teams, risk pipeline for finance or compliance, conversational agent for support.
3. **Match their language.** Technical person: name the tools. Business person: name the outcome. If unsure, say the outcome first and the tool second.
4. **Finish with a question.** An introduction that ends in a question starts a conversation. One that ends in a credential starts an awkward pause.
5. **Keep the ship-design story short.** "I studied naval architecture, then moved to AI" is enough. It makes you memorable. The full story is for a second meeting.
6. **Say "at my current company" for employer projects.** Describe what you built, not the client names, unless you have permission.
7. **Never read from the script.** Learn the shape, not the words: name, credential, what you build, question.

**Three-step practice:** say the default 10-second line out loud. Add one project. Add one question. Stop. That is a complete introduction for any room.
