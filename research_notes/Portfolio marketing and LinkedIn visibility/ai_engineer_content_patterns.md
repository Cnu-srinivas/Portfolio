# What AI engineers and independent AI consultants post that earns visibility and inbound client work (2025–2026)

Scope note: research run 2026-10-11. Sources are 2025–2026 unless marked **[older]**. Most LinkedIn "algorithm" numbers come from tool vendors (MagicPost, AuthoredUp, Buffer, Ordinal, Dataslayer, ViralBrain); they are large-n but not independent, and they measure different things (reach vs engagement vs likes). First-person consultant data comes almost entirely from Jason Liu (jxnl) and Hamel Husain; the Indian creator cohort has almost no published first-person "this post got me a client" data (see Gaps).

---

## Key Question 1 — Who are the AI builders whose content demonstrably brought them clients, what do they post, how often, and which post types produced leads vs followers?

### Takeaway
The only AI practitioners with published, first-person lead numbers are Jason Liu (4–8 inbound leads/month, ~50% close, one $140k deal traced to a single post circulating in a buyer's Slack) and, more qualitatively, Hamel Husain (vendor/consulting inbound "nearly every week" from technical writing). Both say the content that converted was a narrow, problem-specific long-form post ("RAG is more than embeddings"), amplified by high-volume short posts; broad topics grew followers but not clients. The Indian creator cohort (Avi Chawla, Akshay Pachaar, Shubham Saboo, Paul Iusztin, etc.) has large audiences and newsletter businesses, but no published post-to-client attribution.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The pattern across Liu and Husain is identical: one **narrow, owned problem** (RAG for Liu, evals for Husain) written about in depth and repeatedly, distributed through high-frequency short posts. Clients arrive already pre-qualified ("prospects arrived already understanding his expertise").
- Leads came from **long-form being forwarded inside buyer organisations** (Slack), not from the viral post itself. Short posts are the discovery layer; the long-form piece is the sales asset. A LinkedIn-only strategy with no durable long-form home is leaving the conversion asset out.
- The Indian creator cohort's model is audience → newsletter sponsorship/courses; it is a reach model, not a lead model, and should not be copied wholesale by someone selling builds.

## Key Question 2 — Post archetypes with evidence: which get reach from practitioners vs DMs from buyers?

### Takeaway
Buyer-converting archetypes are problem-specific "I solved this" write-ups, anonymised case/outcome posts, and evaluation/failure analyses tied to a decision the buyer is about to make; reach archetypes are launch/tool news, release-note style posts, contrarian one-liners and broad explainers. Buyers' own red-flag lists reward dated, redacted work products and penalise unnamed case studies with no reference.

### Cited Findings
- **Problem-specific build write-up (converts):** Liu's "RAG is more than embeddings" → "over a dozen companies" and "a major driver of consulting work"; "Highly specific titles reach smaller audiences but convert better." — [Indie Consulting](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/01/22/indie-consulting/) **[older]**; [Creating Content That Converts](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/04/04/creating-content-that-converts-my-guide-for-ai-consultants/)
- **Client-call-derived posts (converts):** Liu's recommended source is "common questions, challenges, and the solutions he provided," anonymised; Husain: "most popular posts address common problems from consulting clients." — [Consulting writing](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/10/31/consulting-writing/); [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/audience/)
- **Outcome/case-style posts (converts):** "Case-style posts about specific problems and results"; "Emphasize outcomes over technical details"; "Technical details are often the 'lowest pole of value.'" — [Getting Started](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/10/31/consulting-start/); [Creating Content That Converts](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/04/04/creating-content-that-converts-my-guide-for-ai-consultants/)
- **Title/hook archetypes Liu tests:** numbered benefit lists ("3 ChatGPT Prompts That Doubled My Content Creation Speed"), contrarian claims ("Why your AI services are underpriced"), "mistakes I made." — [Creating Content That Converts](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/04/04/creating-content-that-converts-my-guide-for-ai-consultants/)
- **Evals as a category (converts into training/consulting demand):** Husain's 2025–2026 posts are almost all evals; "'It's Hard to Eval' Is a Product Smell" (Jun 2026), "Do Automated Evals Work?" (Jul 2026), "Stop Saying RAG Is Dead" (Jul 2025) — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/); 2,000+ trained — [Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-ai-evals-are-the-hottest-new-skill)
- **Release-note / "I tested this" posts (reach):** Saboo's "just shipped / now live" style at 7.9 posts/week — [ViralBrain](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/shubham-saboos-high-signal-ai-creator-playbook) (vendor).
- **Satire/personal posts top the reach charts even for data analysts:** van der Blom's top post in Aug–Sep 2026 was AI-slop satire (1,748 likes, 515 comments); his best analysis post ("I've spent 3 weeks buried in algorithm data…") got 557 likes / 286 comments. 78% of his engagers interacted with only one post. — [SignalScout analysis](https://kokasexton.com/insights/richard-van-der-blom.html) (vendor)
- **Buyer-side signals (what turns DMs into trust):** positive signals include "publicly shared, dated, redacted work products," "a one-page data-flow diagram," "redacted sample runbooks or test sheets," "estimates built from your own numbers, with assumptions shown," "admitting uncertainty." Red flags: "case studies that name no business and offer no reference to call," "savings quoted before the consultant has measured your volumes," "a specific tool recommended early," "outdated content suggests outdated proposals." — [Sagnik Bhattacharya, Sep 29 2026](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/ai-consultant-red-flags)
- "Many 'AI engineers' have only Jupyter notebook experience. Ask for a model that's served real users at scale." Ask about "a model that failed in production." — [goLance hiring guide 2026](https://golance.com/hiring/how-to-hire-ai-consultants); "Retainer before roadmap" as a red flag — [Superdupr](https://superdupr.com/blog/ai-consultants-small-business.md)
- **Engineer-audience turn-offs:** "a task took 5 minutes instead of 2 days" posts with no substance; "people just look at high engagement posts and copy the outline/format." — [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/ive-noticed-that-the-upcoming-linkedin-influencers-have-a-certain-post-format-omv3l554); "the people promoting the hype have clearly never created anything of value with it" — [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/ai-advocators-are-not-brilliant-8nwk5ze3); inaccuracies in a traction post raise trust questions — [dev.to](https://dev.to/technoblogger14o3/ai-content-is-everywhere-on-social-media-especially-linkedin-2bbk) (2023–2025 threads, cynical sample).
- **Micro-blogging from DMs (Lenny-ecosystem technique, secondary source):** Ryan Hoover's approach of inviting questions by DM, answering privately, then sharing anonymised screenshots publicly. — [Lenny-skills summary](https://claudeskills.info/skills/refoundai/lenny-skills/personal-brand-network/)

### Inferences
- Mapping to the archetypes in the brief: "one lesson from a real build," "what broke in production," anonymised before/after, cost/latency breakdowns and eval results are the **lead** class (they match what buyers' red-flag lists reward: dated, redacted, number-backed, uncertainty-admitting). Tool comparisons, "I tested X for a week," launch reactions and contrarian one-liners are the **reach** class. Teaching explainers sit in between: they build the follower base that makes lead posts travel.
- Because this author cannot publish client metrics, the buyer-side preference for "estimates built from your own numbers, with assumptions shown" should be satisfied with **public, reproducible numbers** (model list prices, own benchmark runs on public datasets, latency measured on his own demo) rather than client outcomes.
- "What broke in production" posts double as answers to the hiring question "tell me about a model that failed in production," so they pre-answer buyer diligence.

### Gaps
- No dataset tags LinkedIn posts by archetype and measures DMs or booked calls; the lead/reach split above is inferred from consultant testimony plus buyer checklists, not measured.

## Key Question 3 — Hook formulas that work in 2026, what reads as AI slop, length, line breaks, bullets, emojis

### Takeaway
The best-supported 2026 data (MagicPost, ~1.2M posts, within-author) shows templated AI phrasings cost 4–7% reach each, while posts with no concrete detail and no lived experience sit ~23% below the author's average; the first ~150 characters (≈40–50 words) decide the "see more." Longer posts (1,300–3,000 chars) win engagement; one or two emojis help marginally, more does nothing; heavy line-breaking correlates with much higher engagement.

### Cited Findings
**AI-slop tells (within-author reach effect, MagicPost 2026):**
- "Here's what / Here's how" openers (10% of posts): about −4.3%. "It's not X, it's Y" (used by all top creators sometimes): −4.9%. "The result?" cliffhanger: −4.8%. "The key is… / Stop doing X": −6.7% (largest). "Moreover/Furthermore" and "It's worth noting that…": "dead giveaways." Em dash now in 11% of posts (up from <2% pre-AI) but one dash is a weak hint. Closing question and P.S.: no harm. "Let's be honest…" helps (+4.6%) when real candor follows. Substance dominates phrasing: posts "with no concrete detail and no lived experience sat about 23% below the author's average after July 27, 2026." Advice: "add one checkable detail and one real experience first, then fix the phrasing." — [MagicPost, 11 patterns](https://magicpost.in/blog/spot-ai-written-linkedin-post); summary stat "reach drops 3 to 7% per AI turn of phrase" — [MagicPost studies index](https://magicpost.in/studies)
- Recruiter-side detector flags the "vulnerability hook" in which "the failure is tasteful, the lesson is tidy, and nothing concrete actually went wrong," and vocabulary like "resilience," "authentic," "lean into" (vendor claims ~80% of AI-drafted posts). — [AIcheckr](https://www.aicheckr.io/blog/ai-detector-for-linkedin-posts)
- Van der Blom: "about 80% of the comments Richard receives in the first five minutes of any post are written by AI," often from the same accounts. — [Creator Science #307, Jun 2 2026](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/)

**Hook mechanics:**
- The first 150 characters appear before "See more"; recommended specific openers: a data point, a contrarian take, a specific problem. Contrast given: "Excited to share some insights about leadership" vs "I lost my best employee yesterday." — [Dataslayer, Feb 2026](https://dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now) (vendor)
- "The first roughly 60 tokens (about 40–50 words)" carry most weight; "front-load POV, metrics, and specific industry entities"; engagement bait discouraged. — [UNTAMED, Jan 20 2026](https://unfractional.substack.com/p/linkedins-2026-algorithm) (vendor, no primary data)
- Liu: "A mediocre post with a compelling title will outperform a brilliant post with a boring title every time"; test 3–5 framings as short posts first; AIDA for every piece (hook → story/statistic → what's possible → one next step). — [Creating Content That Converts](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/04/04/creating-content-that-converts-my-guide-for-ai-consultants/)
- Claimed but unverified: curiosity-gap/contrarian hooks "2.3X" engagement; hooks under 10 words "+40%"; 61+ s dwell → ~15.6% engagement vs 0–3 s → ~1.2%. — [LinkedInPreview](https://linkedinpreview.com/blog/linkedin-hook-formulas-2026); [Ligo hooks](https://ligosocial.com/blog/linkedin-post-hooks-30-first-line-formulas) (vendor, methodology absent)

**Length / structure:**
- AuthoredUp (372,126 posts, Sep 2025–Feb 2026): 1,301–2,500 characters ≈ +27% engagement vs <400. — via [ViralBrain](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/ideal-linkedin-post-length)
- LigoSocial (2024–2026, same-author comparison): >2,400 chars +24% vs 600–900; <150 chars −31%; "no sweet spot in the middle where the curve turns down." — [LigoSocial](https://ligosocial.com/research/linkedin-post-length)
- Ordinal (248,275 posts, 2024–2026): 2,000–3,000 chars highest engagement rate (1.33%); 600–1,500 weakest (0.81–1.06%). — [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-post-size)
- Shield Index: reach stops growing past ~150 words but engagement keeps rising with length. — [Shield Index](https://theshieldindex.beehiiv.com/p/does-linkedin-post-length-actually-matter)
- Line breaks: among 800–1,200-char posts, 41+ line breaks → 0.675% engagement vs 0.147% for 1–5 breaks. — [Stan](https://stan.store/blog/linkedin-engagement/) (6 months of one dataset)
- Liu's structure: ~5-minute reads, short paragraphs, headers, bullets, bold key points, subheadings every few paragraphs. — [Consulting writing](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/10/31/consulting-writing/) **[older]**
- Husain: write at ~6th-grade reading level; "flowery or jargon-heavy language… AI tends to produce this, so edit for clarity." — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/audience/)

**Emojis:**
- MagicPost (1,201,129 posts): median likes 26 (0 emojis), 32 (1), 34 (2, peak), back to 26 at 5. — [MagicPost emojis](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-emojis)
- AuthoredUp (847k+ personal posts, Mar 2025–Feb 2026): one emoji 2.47% ER and 970 median reach vs 798 for zero; flat from 1 to 15. — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/how-to-add-emojis-on-linkedin-posts)
- Contradicting older study (4.6M posts, undated): 15–16 emojis multiplied the probability of 100 reactions by 2.5. — [Into The Minds](https://www.intotheminds.com/blog/en/linkedin-effect-emojis-emoticons/) **[older, reactions not reach]**

### Inferences
- For a technical builder the safest hook forms are: a measured number from his own run, a named failure mode, a specific decision ("we turned the agent off for X"), or a concrete artefact ("the 9-line stop condition"). Avoid the negate-reframe ("It's not the model, it's the retrieval") and "Here's how" openers that technical creators overuse.
- Length guidance converges on 1,300–2,500 characters with 1–2-line paragraphs; this matches Liu's "5-minute read" structure when the post is the summary of a long-form piece.
- One emoji at most, used as a bullet, is neutral-to-slightly-positive; emoji-heavy "🚀🔥" posts signal the reach class that engineers mock.

### Gaps
- No study ranks specific opening-line templates by reach with disclosed methodology; the "2.3X contrarian" and "65% decide on the first line" figures are unsourced vendor claims.
- All reach-penalty figures are observational (within-author baselines), not causal.

## Key Question 4 — Visual formats: what performs for technical posts in 2026?

### Takeaway
Across four large 2025–2026 datasets, image+text and document/carousel posts lead on reach and engagement, video has the highest engagement in some datasets but lost 36% reach YoY in another and is avoided by van der Blom, text-only trails for most accounts, polls are dead, and link posts lose 20–60% reach. No source measures hand-drawn/Excalidraw diagrams or agent screen recordings specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **Buffer (2M+ posts, 94k accounts):** carousels/PDFs ≈ +278% engagement vs video, +303% vs images, ~+600% vs text; images +72% and video +84% vs text; links in body lowest. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- **AuthoredUp (372K personal posts, Mar 2025–Feb 2026):** reach multipliers — document/carousel 1.39x, poll 1.24x (but 0.37x engagement), image 1.20x, text 1.07x, video 0.86x, article 0.69x. YoY reach: video −36%, images −16%, articles −6%. Image posts are 57.2% of all posts. — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-articles-vs-posts)
- **MagicPost (1.2M posts, updated Jun 5 2026):** median reach — poll 1,154, carousel 1,031, image 823, video 708, text 664, document 544, link 414; median likes — image 34, carousel 34, video 33, text 16, link 10. Carousel slide count: 2–5 slides 22 likes, 9–12 slides 49, 13–20 slides 58. Video: 1–2 min 45 likes, 2–5 min 48, 5+ min 30. Link vs no link median reach 414 vs 795. For accounts under 5k followers: image 15, carousel 15, video 11, text 7 median likes. — [MagicPost formats](https://magicpost.in/blog/best-linkedin-post-format)
- **Dataslayer (Feb 2026, vendor + van der Blom citations):** document/PDF carousel 6.60% avg engagement (highest); native video 30–90 s 5.60%; image+text 3.20%; text ~2%; polls 0.07% ("effectively dead after the March 2026 update"); external link ≈ −60% reach; documents 5–10 slides, engagement drops after slide 10; "completion tracking" of slides; first 60 minutes test with 2–5% of network. — [Dataslayer](https://dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)
- **Van der Blom (1.3M posts H1 2026, via podcast):** prefers text-plus-image and carousels; avoids polls, video, and text-only; text-only posts "average higher reach" but "excluding the top 5% of creators, all their metrics are down… they require exceptional copywriting." — [Creator Science #307](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/)
- **Metricool (577,180 posts, Apr 2025):** carousels 45.85% "engagement rate" (definition unclear; gated report), video posts +53% YoY. — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/linkedin-trends-study-2025/); one-brand test: carousels 48.3% vs video 8.5% because LinkedIn counts every slide click as engagement. — [SEOworks](https://www.seoworks.co.uk/downloads/data-led-analysis-of-linkedin-video-vs-carousels/)
- **Ordinal (900k+ posts):** 26.5% reach penalty for links in the body; link-in-comments "directionally positive but not conclusive." — [Ordinal](https://www.tryordinal.com/blog/linkedin-link-penalty-study)
- **Technical creators' practice:** Husain puts the post title inside the social-card image because X doesn't show titles on large-image cards; uses X threads to "walk through screenshots or snippets." — [hamel.dev](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/audience/)
- Hand-drawn style: described as signalling "a working draft, so engineers and PMs are more willing to use it" — opinion, no performance data. — [besthub review](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/hands-on-review-of-9-ai-architecture-diagram-tools-from-mermaid-beautifier-to-gpt-image2-c6c0995c6dac)

### Inferences
- Default container for a technical post: **text (1,300–2,500 chars) + one image** (diagram, dashboard screenshot, eval table). It is the format with the broadest support across datasets and the lowest production cost.
- Use **carousels of 9–13 slides** for pipeline walkthroughs; the data says short carousels (2–5 slides) underperform single images, so don't make a carousel unless there are 9+ real steps.
- Video is the most contested format (engagement up in MagicPost, reach −36% in AuthoredUp, avoided by van der Blom). Use 1–2-minute screen recordings of an agent only where the motion is the point (turn-taking, tool calls streaming); otherwise a still with annotations.
- Never put the long-form link in the body; link in first comment is a consistent ~11% drag but stable (see Q5).

### Gaps
- No dataset isolates Excalidraw/hand-drawn diagrams, code screenshots (Carbon/Ray.so), or agent screen recordings vs other images.
- Engagement-rate numbers across vendors are not comparable (different denominators; carousel slide-clicks inflate engagement).

## Key Question 5 — How content converts: lead magnets, "comment X and I'll DM," newsletters, office hours, free audits, moving a commenter to a call

### Takeaway
"Comment KEYWORD" lead-magnet posts went from slightly above the author's average to roughly half (−49% to −58%) after July 27, 2026, with median comments per post collapsing from 59.5 to 9.5; "DM me" CTAs also lost reach (−16% → −26%). Closing questions and link-in-first-comment held steady. The conversion mechanisms with first-person evidence are: long-form posts forwarded inside buyer teams, newsletters (van der Blom: ~80% of his conversions), and Liu's "share 2–3 of my own posts in the first call" plus situational-assessment framing.

### Cited Findings
**"Comment X and I'll DM" in 2026 — the single best dataset:**

Impressions on keyword posts +9% → −52%; people reached +14% → −62%. Same-creator comparison (79 creators): 60 dropped, 19 rose; their keyword posts +15% → −48% while their other posts fell only 7%. Median comments per keyword post 59.5 → 9.5; share with more comments than likes 44% → 21%. Recommendations: stop gating resources behind a keyword in the post; end with a genuine question; put the link in the first comment (−11%, stable). No causal claim; vendor sells scheduling. — [MagicPost comment-bait study](https://magicpost.in/studies/linkedin-comment-bait)
- Engagement bait ("Agree? Comment 👇") described as penalised after a "March 2026 Authenticity Update" — unsourced vendor claim. — [Dataslayer](https://dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now)
- A CTA guide says "Comment X and I will DM you the doc" still works "only when the payoff is real and the topic is narrow." — [LigoSocial CTA examples](https://ligosocial.com/blog/linkedin-call-to-action-examples) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; claim from search summary)

- Buyer side: "Paid discovery that ends in a written output you keep" and "a small paid pilot for consultants with few references" are positive signals; free "guaranteed ROI" is a red flag. — [Bhattacharya](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/ai-consultant-red-flags)

### Inferences
- For an author who must avoid "open to work" signals, the 2026 data argues for **ungated artefacts** (checklist, eval notebook, stop-condition template) linked in the first comment or hosted on the site, with a closing question in the post. The keyword-gate mechanic is now both a reach penalty and a credibility signal of the reach-class creator.
- The strongest conversion asset in the evidence is a **newsletter or long-form home** that a buyer's team can forward; the LinkedIn newsletter product specifically has reach and click tracking advantages in 2026.
- A "paid discovery / written assessment" offer, mentioned in the P.S. of lead-class posts, matches both Liu's situational-assessment close and buyers' positive-signal list, and does not read as job-seeking.

### Gaps
- No measured conversion data for "office hours" or "free audit" posts was found; all sources were booking pages without numbers.
- No data on DM volume or booked calls per post type exists publicly; the MagicPost study measures reach/comments only.

## Key Question 7 — Series and recurring formats vs one-offs

### Takeaway
No dataset compares labelled series against one-offs; the indirect evidence (topic consistency improves the 2026 interest-graph placement, higher posting frequency raises per-post reach, Liu's RAG series and Husain's multi-year evals series were their conversion engines) favours a small number of named recurring threads, while sponsored-post data warns that hammering the same topic too often fatigues audiences.

### Cited Findings
- Van der Blom: LinkedIn moved to an interest graph; "paste your last 20 posts into an AI tool and ask for the main topics. Aim for one main topic and a few related subtopics, with about 80% of content on them"; within three weeks of re-focusing his profile he saw more profile visits and reach. Optimal frequency fell from 5–6/week to 2–4; irregular "when I have something to share" posters saw reach +10–15%. — [Creator Science #307](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/)
- Buffer (2M+ posts): vs once a week, 2–5 posts/week → +1,182 impressions/post and +0.23 pp engagement; 6–10 → +5,001 and +0.76 pp; 11+ → +16,946 and +1.40 pp; held across account sizes. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/); AuthoredUp: 4–5 posts/week → 28% more impressions per post than once a week. — via [Buffer search summary](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-linkedin/)
- Dataslayer: "One valuable weekly post is said to outperform five weak ones." — [Dataslayer](https://dataslayer.ai/blog/linkedin-algorithm-february-2026-whats-working-now) (conflicts with Buffer's volume finding)
- Liu built a RAG series and mined "about 17 potential topics each Friday"; "Volume negates luck, and these efforts compound quickly." — [Creating Content That Converts](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/04/04/creating-content-that-converts-my-guide-for-ai-consultants/); [Marketing](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/05/29/marketing-consulting/)
- Fatigue signal (sponsored posts, not organic): creator posts within two weeks of the last underperformed; ~a month apart was the sweet spot; "audiences can be easily fatigued if creators drill them on the same topics repeatedly." — [Favikon/HubSpot case](https://www.favikon.com/blog/hubspot-influencer-marketing-case-study-how-hubspot-works-with-linkedin-creators)
- Recurring theme advice from a 20k-follower creator: keep "a recurring theme each week, fortnight, or month, since audiences then know what to expect." — [Anangsha](https://anangsha.substack.com/p/i-hit-20k-followers-on-linkedin)
- Sumanth-style daily cadence: Saboo at 7.9 posts/week. — [ViralBrain](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/shubham-saboos-high-signal-ai-creator-playbook)

### Inferences
- A named series ("Production AI failure #N," "Cost of an agent #N") gives the interest graph a consistent topic fingerprint and gives buyers a forwardable index; rotate 2–3 series rather than one to avoid the fatigue effect, and keep ~80% of posts inside the agentic/RAG/voice/evals cluster.
- Target 2–4 posts/week (van der Blom) rather than daily; daily volume is the reach-class pattern and Buffer's +11/week lift is dominated by media accounts.

### Gaps
- No study tags series posts; the question is unanswered with data.

## Key Question 8 — What NOT to post (junior/generic signals, buyer turn-offs)

### Takeaway
Buyers and senior engineers converge: no unnamed case study without something checkable, no tool recommended before scoping, no "5 minutes instead of 2 days" hype, no copied engagement-post skeletons, no outdated product facts, and no AI-templated phrasing without a concrete detail; the 2026 data adds that keyword-gated lead magnets and polls now read as reach-class tactics and are penalised.

### Inferences
- Junior signals specific to this author's niche: basic LangChain/LlamaIndex tutorials, model-launch reaction posts, "10 AI tools" listicles, reposted benchmark screenshots with no own run, and any "excited to announce" framing. Each matches a flagged pattern above (copied skeleton, no lived experience, reach-class).
- "Open to work" leakage to avoid: headline changes, "available for projects," "DM me for collaborations" closers (the "DM me" CTA is also the worst-performing CTA in 2026 data).

### Gaps
- No survey of CTOs/founders about which LinkedIn post types made them hire; evidence is checklists written by consultants and anonymous engineer forums.

## Catalogue — 36 post ideas/templates for a senior AI engineer (LLM agents, RAG, voice AI, document AI, evals in production) who cannot name employer/products/clients, cannot invent metrics, and must not signal "open to work"

### Takeaway
Every template below uses only four kinds of evidence the author is allowed to publish: (a) **public numbers** (model list prices, public benchmark results, own measurements on public data or his own demo), (b) **redacted artefacts** (eval sheets, runbooks, stop-condition tables, data-flow diagrams with generic labels), (c) **qualitative, dated lived experience** ("in one deployment last quarter…") with no client-identifying detail, and (d) **decisions and trade-offs**. Each is tagged LEAD (buyer-facing, converts) or REACH (peer-facing, grows the base), with the evidence it rests on. Nothing below repeats the earlier "stop conditions first" post, the six follow-ups, or the warm-outreach message.

### Cited Findings
- Format defaults used in the catalogue: text 1,300–2,500 chars + one image; carousel only at 9+ slides; link in first comment; closing question; no keyword gate; ≤1 emoji; 2–4 posts/week; ~80% inside one topic cluster. — [AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-articles-vs-posts); [MagicPost formats](https://magicpost.in/blog/best-linkedin-post-format); [MagicPost comment-bait](https://magicpost.in/studies/linkedin-comment-bait); [MagicPost emojis](https://magicpost.in/blog/linkedin-emojis); [Creator Science #307](https://creatorscience.com/podcast/richard-van-der-blom-2/)
- Hook rules used: lead with a specific number, named failure, decision or artefact; no "Here's how," "It's not X, it's Y," "The key is," "Stop doing X," "The result?" — [MagicPost 11 patterns](https://magicpost.in/blog/spot-ai-written-linkedin-post)
- Positioning rules used: name the problem, sell the outcome, share redacted work product, state assumptions, admit uncertainty. — [Liu, Getting Started](https://jxnl.co/writing/2024/10/31/consulting-start/); [Bhattacharya](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/ai-consultant-red-flags)
- Anonymisation rule used: "N systems across industries," "a team I worked with," recorded-with-permission insights, no logos. — [Liu, Creating Content That Converts](https://jxnl.co/writing/2025/04/04/creating-content-that-converts-my-guide-for-ai-consultants/)

### Inferences (the catalogue)

**Series A — "Production AI failure of the week" (LEAD; recurring, 1 image: a redacted trace or a two-box diagram)**
1. **The silent tool-call loop.** Hook: "An agent made 212 identical tool calls before anyone noticed. The trace looked healthy." Body: how the loop hid inside retries, the three detectors added (cycle hash, token budget, wall-clock), what changed in the runbook. No client; number is from the author's own reproduction on a public agent framework, stated as such.
2. **Retrieval that was correct and useless.** Hook: "Top-5 recall was fine. The answer was still wrong." Body: chunk boundaries cut a table header from its rows; fix = structure-aware chunking; include a before/after of a public PDF (e.g., a government form) rather than client docs.
3. **The eval that passed because the judge was lenient.** Hook: "Our LLM judge gave 94% pass. Humans gave 61%." Body: calibration method (small labelled set, agreement stats), what a judge prompt must include; numbers from a public dataset run he does himself.
4. **Voice agent that talked over the caller.** Hook: "The caller paused to think. The agent took it as a turn." Body: endpointing thresholds, barge-in handling, what was measured in the author's own test harness (seconds, not business outcomes).
5. **Document AI that hallucinated a field.** Hook: "The invoice had no PO number. The model found one anyway." Body: schema-constrained extraction, 'null is a valid answer' training, confidence routing to a human queue.
6. **The fallback that never fired.** Hook: "We had a fallback model configured for eight months. It had never been invoked." Body: why (health check tested the wrong endpoint), the synthetic failure drill now in the checklist.

**Series B — "Cost of an agent #N" (LEAD; public pricing math; 1 image: a 6-row cost table)**
7. **Price a support agent per resolved ticket from list prices.** Hook: "At list price, one agent turn with a 40k-token context costs more than the human it replaces for simple tickets." Body: show the arithmetic on public model prices, where caching and routing change the answer; invite readers to plug in their volumes. Assumptions stated; no client volumes.
8. **Voice minute economics.** Hook: "Four line items decide whether a voice agent is cheaper than a call centre: STT, LLM, TTS, telephony." Body: public per-minute prices, latency budget that forces model choice.
9. **RAG cost: re-embedding vs re-ranking.** Hook: "Re-embedding a 2M-chunk corpus is a budget line. A reranker is a latency line. Pick one deliberately." Body: cost/latency table from public prices and the author's own benchmark.
10. **Eval cost is a product line, not overhead.** Hook: "A 500-example eval run on a frontier model costs about the same as one engineer-hour. Run it on every PR." Body: the budget rule he uses; numbers from public pricing.

**Series C — "One decision from a real build" (LEAD; short, decision-centred, qualitative)**
11. **Why we didn't give the agent write access on day one.** Hook: "We shipped a read-only agent first. Six weeks later it earned write access — one tool at a time." Body: the graduation criteria (eval pass rate, incident-free window, human-review sampling).
12. **When fine-tuning lost to a better retrieval index.** Hook: "The fine-tune request was approved. We cancelled it after a two-day retrieval fix." Body: how the decision was tested; no customer named.
13. **Why structured outputs replaced a 600-word prompt.** Hook: "A JSON schema deleted two-thirds of our prompt." Body: what the schema enforces that instructions could not.
14. **Why we route 30% of documents to humans on purpose.** Hook: "A document pipeline that never asks a human is a document pipeline nobody audited." Body: confidence thresholds, reviewer queue design, how the threshold moves as evals improve (describe the mechanism; do not publish client throughput).

**Series D — "Redacted artefact" posts (LEAD; the buyer-checklist format: dated, redacted work product; image = the artefact)**
15. **The agent runbook page one.** A screenshot of a real runbook template (own template, generic labels): triggers, kill switch, owner, rollback. Hook: "This is page one of every agent runbook I write."
